\begin{abstract}
Distributed LLM inference on heterogeneous edge clusters faces a
trade-off between placement that maximizes throughput and redundancy
that survives failure. \sys{} unifies layer placement~($\psi$) and
recovery routing~($R$) in a single alternating dynamic program whose
backup-memory reservation folds back into the placement constraint.

We pair the optimizer with two runtime mechanisms: an
\emph{asynchronous mirror cache} that ships per-stage activations to
the coordinator out-of-band so a promoted backup can rebuild its KV
state, and \emph{asynchronous chain forwarding} with a
\texttt{ResultReady} reverse channel that breaks the synchronous
nested-response unwind we found silently serializes nominally
pipelined chains.

Live measurement on a six-worker Jetson fleet (with an AGX Xavier
coordinator, Jetson-only deployment) covers \emph{24}
operating points on OPT-350M (two chain lengths $\times$ four
architecture variants $\times$ three concurrency levels) plus a
nine-cell 4-stage sub-matrix on a 3$\times$-larger Llama-3.2-1B. Latency-optimized placement dominates throughput-
optimized placement at every point with concurrency $C \geq 4$ on
both models; the only measured exception is single-stream
($C{=}1$) Llama-3.2-1B, where throughput placement wins on aggregate
tokens-per-second while latency placement still wins on
token-between-time. Asynchronous chain forwarding adds a chain-
length-independent 17--47\% throughput gain at $C{=}16$ without
reversing the placement ordering. Under a worker SIGKILL mid-stream,
\sys{} delivers 60/60 coherent tokens; placement-only baselines
abort after the 17--20 already-buffered tokens.
\end{abstract}
