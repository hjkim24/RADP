\section{Related Work}\label{sec:related}

Petals~\cite{borzunov2023petals,borzunov2023distributed}
demonstrated collaborative LLM inference on consumer hardware with
greedy placement and swarm-redundancy-based recovery, but its
16+\,GB peer assumption excludes the 4\,GB Nano regime \sys{}
targets. EdgeShard~\cite{zhang2024edgeshard} splits the
placement problem into two algorithms --- $O(N \cdot M^2)$ latency
mode (Algorithm~1) and $O(N^2 \cdot 2^M \cdot M^2)$ throughput
mode (Algorithm~2) --- but each algorithm is reported only against
its own objective, so the relative behaviour of latency-optimal
vs throughput-optimal placement under concurrent load is not
measured. Jupiter~\cite{ye2025jupiter} pushes the
throughput-optimal end with a single max-min DP (Eq.~1) plus
explicit TBT-SLO constraints, achieved on top of intra-sequence
pipeline parallelism; the placement DP itself is the algorithmic
ancestor of our recurrence (Eq.~\ref{eq:dp} at $\alpha{=}1$
without recovery or $\gamma_{\mathrm{hop}}$). Neither system
provides a recovery story, neither models per-hop framing/GIL
overhead in the DP, and neither cross-evaluates latency vs
throughput placement on the same metric --- the gap the 24-cell
matrix in \S\ref{sec:eval:matrix} fills.
Helix~\cite{mei2025helix} measures greedy placement's
2.38$\times$ throughput tax on heterogeneous GPU fleets via a
max-flow formulation, motivating DP-based approaches more
generally. \sys{} unifies these threads: the same DP yields
EdgeShard's latency-mode and Jupiter's throughput-mode placement
via the $\alpha$ knob, recovery is co-optimised through
Eq.~\ref{eq:mem} rather than bolted on, and the asynchronous
mirror cache plus chain-aware recovery add fault tolerance at the
4\,GB-per-peer edge frontier where prior swarm-redundancy
recipes overflow.
